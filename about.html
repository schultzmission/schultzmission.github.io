About Our Ministry

Who We Are

We're Ben and Brianna Schultz, engineers serving Christ through our ministry at Karanda Mission Hospital in rural Zimbabwe.

Brianna is a Civil Engineer and Ben is a Mechanical Engineer. Our combined skillset allows us to support the growth of the hospital's critical infrastructure and maintain the incredible ministry opportunities it creates. We both love the work we get to do at Karanda, and the people we get to meet through the hospital.

Our Mission

We desire to honor God by using the engineering gifts He has given us to serve Karanda Mission Hospital and its mission of providing affordable, reliable, and compassionate healthcare to rural Zimbabwe. Through our work and relationships, we seek to demonstrate the love of Christ and clearly share the hope of the Gospel.

We are appointed with the mission organization TEAM (team.org) as long term global workers.
We are grateful to be supported and sent by our home church Lighthouse Community (mylighthousecommunity.com)

While supporting the hospital through our engineering work demands much of our time, we have also encountered many people who are eager to understand God's Word and grow in their faith. We are investing in discipleship and Bible teaching, seeking to equip people to study Scripture for themselves and develop a deeper, more biblically grounded understanding of the Gospel.

Our Calling

Brianna

Missions has been part of my life for as long as I can remember...

Read Brianna's story

Both of my parents were teachers at the private Christian high school I attended, and part of their role was leading a mission trip for high school students each year. As their daughter, I was able to tag along from a very young age. By the time I turned 18, I had been on a mission trip almost every year of my life.

Those trips shaped the way I saw the world. From an early age, I saw that people in other parts of the world lived very differently from the way we did in America. I saw families facing needs that I had never experienced myself, and I began to wonder if there was more I could do with my life than simply live and work in the comfort I had always known.

One need in particular stood out to me: water.

I remember seeing people walk long distances to collect water and then carry it all the way back home. Even as a young child, seeing something as basic as obtaining clean water require so much time and effort stayed with me. I began thinking about how I could use my life and education to help meet needs like that.

When it came time to choose a college major, I knew I wanted to do something that could help provide clean water to people. I had always enjoyed math and science, so civil engineering seemed like a natural fit. I chose it with the hope that someday I could use what I learned to help solve real problems for people who needed it most.

I didn’t know it at the time, but God was already preparing the pieces that would eventually lead me to Zimbabwe.

In college, my civil engineering senior design project gave me the opportunity to partner with Karanda Mission Hospital in rural Zimbabwe. We were asked to help develop a water filtration system for the hospital, a project that brought together two things that had been part of my life for years: engineering and missions.

What began as a college engineering project eventually became the doorway through which God brought Karanda into our lives.

Ben

Growing up, I had opportunities to see missions firsthand through several short-term mission trips with my youth group. But I never really saw myself as a future missionary…

Read Ben's story

What did stand out to me from an early age was the way God had wired me. I loved figuring out how things worked, taking things apart, fixing them, and working with my hands. Engineering was something I was good at, and something I genuinely enjoyed.

As I got older, I began to see those abilities as gifts from God that I could use to serve other people. I enjoyed the practical side of serving: building something, fixing something that was broken, or using my skills to solve a problem that someone else couldn’t solve on their own.

I was never opposed to missions. I just didn’t see how my passion for engineering fit with being a missionary.

When I thought about missions, I pictured people whose primary role was preaching, teaching, or working directly in ministry. I didn’t see where an engineer belonged. I assumed I would use my engineering skills in a career and perhaps use them on short-term mission trips along the way.

That began to change when Brianna and I were in college and her senior design project partnered with Karanda Mission Hospital in Zimbabwe.

For the first time, I saw a different picture of what missions could look like. The hospital needed people who could fix things, understand systems, and work alongside the people already serving there. Our time at Karanda opened my eyes to how God had been preparing me long before I ever considered becoming a missionary. The skills and interests I had always seen as simply part of who I was could be used to serve the Christ and meet very real needs on the mission field.

Looking back, I can see that God was bringing together two things I had not imagined would meet: my passion for engineering and His call to make disciples and serve others.

Our first trip to Karanda in 2024 was where I began to see that those things didn’t have to be separate.

Together

While we were in college, Brianna's senior design project partnered with Karanda to develop a water filtration system...

Read our story

What started as an engineering project became an opportunity to see firsthand how God could use our skills for something much bigger than a career.

After we got married, we traveled to Karanda to install the system and spent several weeks living and working alongside the hospital staff. We worked together in the maintenance shop, shared meals, played volleyball in the evenings, and began building friendships with people from a completely different part of the world. More than anything, we saw the way God was using Karanda Mission Hospital to care for people, not only through affordable and compassionate healthcare, but through the hope of the Gospel.

When we returned home, we expected life to go back to normal. But Karanda never really left our hearts.

We continued praying for the hospital and staying in contact with friends there. Even from Ohio, God continued to give us opportunities to help, whether through engineering projects, finding parts and equipment, or simply encouraging the people we had come to know. At the same time, we began to recognize how He had been preparing us through our engineering careers. Brianna’s work in water and wastewater engineering and Ben’s work in manufacturing and mechanical engineering were giving us skills that could be used in a place where reliable infrastructure and equipment are essential to the ministry of the hospital.

Over time, what had started as a short-term trip began to look more like a calling.

We prayed, talked, sought counsel, and asked God to make His direction clear. Eventually, we felt that He was leading us to leave our jobs and our home in Ohio and return to Karanda to serve as long-term missionaries.

Our calling is not ultimately to engineering or even to Zimbabwe. It is to follow Christ wherever He leads us and to use the gifts He has given us for His purposes. At Karanda, engineering is one of the ways God has opened doors for us to serve. Through maintaining equipment, improving infrastructure, working alongside hospital staff, and solving everyday problems, we have the opportunity to build relationships and point people toward the One who has called us.

We don’t know exactly where every step will lead, but we are grateful for the way God has led us this far. What began as a senior design project became a friendship with a hospital, and ultimately a calling to serve Christ in Africa.

Karanda Mission Hospital: Serving God Serving Others

Karanda Mission Hospital demonstrates Christ's love by providing essential healthcare to patients in need from not only across remote Zimbabwe but also from neighboring Mozambique, Zambia, and Botswana. With 150 beds, it handles 3,800 surgeries each year over 2,000 deliveries, and treats various conditions, including HIV/AIDS. In 2025 the hospital provided almost 140,000 outpatient services and had 7,500 inpatient admissions.

Learn more about Karanda Mission Hospital at www.karanda.org

Engineering & Ministry

We love to share the way God has connected our desires, skills, and passions to the mission field.

Missions can take many forms. God gives each person different gifts and abilities, and He calls us to steward those gifts for His glory and the good of others (1 Corinthians 12:4–7). Not everyone is called to exclusively preach or teach. Just as the body of Christ has many members with different functions, the Church needs people to serve through the gifts God has given them (1 Corinthians 12:12–27). For us, that calling has taken the form of engineering at Karanda.

We believe God has given us these skills for a purpose, and because He has called us to serve here, we are willing to go. Engineering may not fit the traditional picture of missions, but faithfully using the gifts God has given us is an essential way of participating in His mission. From the beginning, God has called humanity to cultivate and steward His creation (Genesis 1:28), and we seek to honor Him by working wholeheartedly with the skills He has entrusted to us (Colossians 3:23–24).
