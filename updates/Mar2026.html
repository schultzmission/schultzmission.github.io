BEN + BRI

#4 | MARCH 2026

Monthly Newsletter

Community Outreach

     An exciting development has been the reinstatement of a community outreach program called “Fun Fridays.” It’s a simple idea, just creating a space for anyone who wants to step away and come spend the afternoon together. It’s been such a joy to see people gather, connect, and enjoy time as a community.

One highlight was attempting to organize a kickball game with the kids… which quickly turned into about 20 kids all running around the bases with Brianna! It may not have gone according to plan, but it was full of laughter and energy.

We’ve also had neighboring villages come out and join in, even bringing their own volleyball teams. That has led to some great (and competitive!) games, adding another layer of fun and connection to these afternoons. It’s been a great reminder that sometimes the simplest ideas can create the most meaningful moments.

Because we live right next to the volleyball court, we’ve also started keeping soccer balls and volleyballs at our house. Throughout the day, groups of kids will come by to check out a ball and then return it later in the evening. It’s become a regular rhythm, and such a fun way to interact with them. These small moments have turned into meaningful opportunities to build relationships and get to know many of the kids better.

 

What We Have Been Working On:

    This past month has been both productive and encouraging for us. One of the biggest highlights was spending time with a missionary couple who previously served here and returned home last year. They recently came back to visit, and it has been incredibly valuable to learn directly from their experience. Hearing how they navigated the same roles we are now stepping into has helped bring clarity to many of our questions, both practical and logistical. It has given us a better sense of direction and confidence in how to move forward in our work here.

We were also blessed by a visit from a missionary who was raised at the Hospital and went on to work here for years. We were able to spend quite a bit of time with him, including going on some pretty crazy hikes on Saturday afternoons. Those times were not only fun, but also meaningful as we heard more of his experiences and perspective from years of serving here.

Because of all this input and encouragement, we’ve been able to take some exciting next steps. We have officially begun designing a new settling tank down by the river. This project is something we’ve been working toward for a while, and it feels good to finally move from ideas into actual planning and design. There is still a lot to consider and work through, but it’s encouraging to see progress and to know that what we’re doing will have a tangible impact.

Alongside the technical work, we’ve also been investing time in building relationships with the maintenance team. These daily interactions have opened doors for deeper conversations, not just about work, but about life and faith as well. Getting to know them personally has been an important and meaningful part of our time here.

We’ve also been really encouraged by the different short-term teams that come through. They’ve brought a sense of friendship and a bit of familiarity from home, which has meant a lot to us. It’s been refreshing to connect with others, share stories, and feel that sense of community, even for a short time.

 

Spiritual Life:


Brianna has continued to serve faithfully with Awana each Sunday, and this past week marked the final meeting for the school term. It was a really special time, as the kids participated in verse quizzes and were recognized for all the hard work they’ve put in over the past months. Some of them memorized all 13 verses from the term, which was amazing to see.

At the end, they were given a few prize options to choose from, and what stood out to us most was that many of the kids chose a Bible. That was such an encouraging moment, seeing their desire to have and engage with God’s Word. It was a reminder that even small, consistent efforts can have a lasting impact.

As we continue building relationships with the maintenance team, we’ve also been learning more about where each person stands spiritually. Some of the men are strong believers, which has been a great encouragement. Others, however, place their faith and trust in a “prophet,” which has brought new challenges and opportunities for conversation.

This has required us to adjust how we approach sharing our faith. The way we might typically have conversations in America doesn’t always translate directly here. Instead, we are learning to engage more thoughtfully, addressing different beliefs and perspectives with patience and care. It has stretched us, but it’s also deepening our understanding of what it means to meet people where they are and point them toward truth.

We’re continuing to pray for wisdom in these conversations and for open hearts. We’re grateful for the opportunities we’ve been given, not just to serve practically, but to be part of what God is doing in people’s lives here.

 

Challenges:

     At times, this life can feel isolating. When the short-term teams head home and the missionaries who come to visit get back on the plane, it often leaves just the two of us who fully understand the life we came from. Those moments can be difficult, and we’re learning how to navigate that reality.

At the same time, we’ve been encouraged by the relationships that are forming around us. There is almost always someone to stop and talk with as we walk around the compound, and those small, everyday interactions have become really meaningful.

Even just this past Saturday, as we were heading out for a hike, a group of about 10 kids suddenly tagged along just to see what we were up to. It turned into such a fun and unexpected time together, and it was a good reminder to us that we are not alone out here. There is community here—we’re just continuing to grow into it.

We’re thankful for both the challenges and the encouragements, and for how God is using both to shape our time here.
