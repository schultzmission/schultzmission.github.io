BEN + BRI

#5 APRIL 2026

Monthly Newsletter

Community Outreach

   

Karanda Mission Hospital serves 43 surrounding villages, representing approximately 2,100 families. This month, we had the privilege of installing boreholes in two of these nearby communities, providing close access to clean, safe drinking water.

In many of these villages, women and children typically walk several kilometers each day to collect water often carrying over 40 pounds back to their homes for cooking, laundry, bathing, and drinking. Being able to bring water closer to home makes a significant difference in daily life.

Before each installation, we inform the community and invite anyone who would like to come and help. This gives people the opportunity to take ownership of the borehole and feel pride in what is being built. At times, no one comes and the work takes much longer but this month, God provided more than enough hands in both villages. It was a joy to work alongside community members as we installed the pumps together.

After installation, we spend time teaching about sanitation and hygiene, helping ensure the water remains safe and beneficial for the whole community. We also have the opportunity to share the Gospel in their own language.

We often use water as a picture: just as we must return day after day because we become thirsty again, Jesus offers something greater. He is the Living Water always present, always faithful and in Him, we find a lasting source that never runs dry.


 

What We Have Been Working On:

As the rainy season has begun to slow down, we’ve shifted our focus to repairing the roads. Heavy rains over the past months have washed out sections, leaving them extremely bumpy and at times, even dangerous to drive on.

Recently, we’ve spent several days filling these washed-out areas by shoveling dirt into the holes. Thankfully, the hospital dug a large pit for a new trash incinerator, and we’ve been able to use that dirt to help restore the roads. We load up a trailer and haul the dirt to the areas that need it most, then spread it by hand.

These repairs are important, as they allow us to safely transport supplies to the villages that need them. It’s slow, physical work but it makes a tangible difference.

On a lighter note, Ben has been learning how to operate the skid steer to help load the trailer. He’s been wanting to drive one for a good portion of his life, so that has been a fun (and helpful) gift from God to get to watch him do that.

It’s been exhausting, and most days we come home completely worn out but we’re grateful to be able to serve in practical ways that support the larger mission.


 

Spiritual Life:

     Easter looked a little different for us this year, but it was still very special. We were able to attend an English-speaking church service in Harare, and the sermon was phenomenal. We also had the chance to organize a small Easter egg hunt for some of the kids, which was a fun way to celebrate together.

This month has been filled with meaningful opportunities to share and witness how God is already at work.

While installing a water pump in Chirongwa Village, during a tea break, Brianna had the chance to meet the village elder and talk with him about Christ. She shared her faith and explained why God has called her and Ben to Zimbabwe. In return, the village elder shared his own testimony. It was deeply encouraging to hear that the leader of the village is a strong believer who desires for others in his community to know God.

Another impactful moment came after a long and exhausting day of road work. As Brianna was walking home, some of the same children who had been asking about her Bible earlier stopped her and asked, “What is the password to get into heaven?” This opened the door to share that there is no password or good works that can earn our way to heaven. We are not perfect and cannot do anything to get into heaven on our own. We need Jesus. He came to earth lived a perfect life, and then died on the cross to pay the punishment for everything that we have done wrong so that we could live with Him in heaven for eternity.

It was a lot for the kids to take in. When asked if they wanted to pray to Jesus, they said they wanted to think about it. We would greatly appreciate prayers for them, that God would continue working in their hearts and drawing them to Himself.

Another exciting opportunity has come through the food packs we are beginning to distribute. Ben has written a gospel tract (a small booklet that clearly shares the message of Christ), and is working with the Awana leader to have it translated into Shona so it can be read and understood by many more people.

We are continually reminded that God is moving in both the big and small moments, and we are grateful to be part of what He is doing
