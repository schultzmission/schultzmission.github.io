BEN + BRI

#6 MAY 2026

Monthly Newsletter

Community Outreach

    This year has been a particularly difficult one for local agriculture and food security. The season began with no rains during January and February, followed by unusually heavy rainfall in March and April. This combination has made it challenging for many families to plant, grow, and harvest crops in a reliable way, and has increased pressure on household food supplies.

In response, we have started providing monthly food packs to 28 families whose children regularly attend Awana faithfully. These families were carefully assessed and identified as being in the greatest need of support at this time. Each food pack includes basic staples and household essentials such as corn, flour, sugar, salt, soap, and toothpaste, helping to cover both nutritional and hygiene needs within the home.

Although there was an intention to keep this initiative quiet, word has spread quickly and we now have people coming to our doorstep every day, sometimes multiple times a day, asking for assistance. This has required us to be thoughtful and consistent in how we respond, ensuring that help is given in a fair and sustainable way.

We truly believe that God knew far better than we did what we would encounter this year, and in His provision He brought in more resources than we needed ahead of time. Because of this and because of your generosity, we have been able to respond to needs as they arise without fear of running short.

As we continue to engage with these requests, we are trying to seek Christ in every situation and be wise and discerning about who we are able to provide financial assistance to. At times, this means we are able to help directly, and at other times we are not in a position to give money. These decisions are never taken lightly, and saying no can be extremely hard. However, when we are unable to provide financial help, we still take time to speak with people, pray with them, and encourage them as best we can. In some cases, rather than giving money, we also look for ways to offer work opportunities or directly provide food and essential items where appropriate.

 

What We Have Been Working On:

    We have recently introduced a weekly coordination meeting with the Maintenance Administrator and the Project Coordinator. This has become an important space where we sit down together and review ongoing projects in more detail. It has also created a useful opportunity to share updates that may not yet have reached everyone across teams, helping to improve overall communication and alignment. Over time, this has started to make it easier to identify gaps early and keep work moving more smoothly between departments.

Ben has been heavily involved in ongoing tree clearing operations in highly constrained environments. A lot of this work involves removing trees that are surrounded by infrastructure such as buildings, boundary fences, and overhead electrical lines. Because of these tight conditions, each job requires careful planning and precise execution to ensure the tree falls safely and in the correct direction. It is methodical work that can be slow at times, but it is essential for maintaining both safety and access in these areas.

On the water operations side, the Head of the Water Department has been on leave for the past month and a half. During this period, Brianna has stepped into his role and taken on his responsibilities. This has included early mornings, waking up at 5:30am to send water to the station (so fun) as well as coordinating the day-to-day work of the water team. She has been guiding the team through required tasks, helping to troubleshoot operational issues, and ensuring continuity in water delivery and system performance while the department head is away.

In addition to these operational duties, we have also submitted plans for a new river settling tank. Brianna is currently working with both the hospital administration and the construction crew to coordinate the next steps. This involves aligning on approvals, logistics, and timelines so that all parties are prepared ahead of construction, and the project can move forward as smoothly as possible once it begins.



 

Spiritual Life:

  The last week of May was spent away from Karanda, and it was a much-needed time of rest and renewal. Living and serving here is a tremendous blessing, but it also means that we are constantly pouring ourselves out for the people around us. Whether through ministry, community outreach, hospital projects, maintenance work, or simply responding to the needs that seem to appear at our doorstep every day, there is always another opportunity to serve.

While we are grateful for these opportunities, constantly pouring out can be draining if we are not intentional about spending time being filled back up ourselves. This week away gave us the chance to slow down, step back from our normal responsibilities, and spend uninterrupted time with the Lord. It allowed us to rest physically, mentally, and spiritually.

One of the reminders that stood out to us during this time is that ministry is not sustained by our own strength, effort, or determination. It is sustained by abiding in Christ. We cannot continually pour into others if we are not first spending time with Him and allowing Him to pour into us.

We returned to Karanda feeling refreshed, encouraged, and grateful for the opportunity to step away for a short time. Thank you for praying for us. Your prayers, encouragement, and support make it possible for us to continue serving here, and we are thankful for the ways God uses you to strengthen us from afar.


 

Victoria Falls!!!


At the end of May, we had the opportunity to meet up with Ben's parents in Victoria Falls, Zimbabwe. This was the first time in nearly six months that we had spent time with people from our own culture, and the fact that it was family made it even more special. We did not realize how much we had missed familiar faces, familiar conversations, and simply being around people who understood our background and experiences. It was a wonderful and refreshing week together, and a much-needed reminder of home.

We are also so incredibly grateful to Ben's parents for making the long journey to come visit us. Living overseas has many joys, but being far from family can be one of the hardest parts. Their willingness to travel all the way to Zimbabwe to spend time with us was a tremendous encouragement, and the memories we made together are something we will cherish for a long time.

We went on safaris and were blessed to see African wildlife in their natural habitat. One of the highlights was getting to see lions up close (maybe too close).

Of course, no trip to Victoria Falls would be complete without seeing the falls themselves. As you get closer, it is not like a light mist drifting through the air, it feels like walking straight into a storm. The spray is so intense that we were completely soaked within minutes. Standing there and witnessing the sheer power and beauty of God's creation was incredible. It is one of those places that makes you feel very small and leaves you in awe of what God has made.
