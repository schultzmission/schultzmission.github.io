BEN + BRI

#8 JULY 2026

Monthly Newsletter

Community Outreach

 

This month has been packed full of short-term visitors! We have had our hands full with people coming to Karanda to serve alongside the ministry here, and while it has kept us busy, it has been a huge blessing.

At the beginning of July, the family that had been hosting the orphan parties headed home, and shortly after, a couple arrived who had previously lived at Karanda for two years. It has been great getting to hear their stories and learn from their experience living here. They also shared one very important piece of information with us: there is an ice cream shop only 1 hour away instead of 3 hours away! Needless to say, we were pretty excited about that discovery.

Also visiting this month is a medical student who has been shadowing doctors around the hospital. He has been a lot of fun to have around and has even helped us with our Friday night young adult gatherings. We quickly learned that popping popcorn for a house full of young adults is basically a full-time job!

We were also blessed to have Brianna's family come visit us here in Zimbabwe (more on that later!), and two professors from Cedarville University's engineering department stopped by to scope out a future engineering project for Karanda.

It has been a busy month trying to make all of our visitors feel welcome while also getting pulled away throughout the day for maintenance projects and hospital emergencies, but we are grateful for everyone who has taken the time to come and serve alongside the ministry here.

 

What We Have Been Working On:

    Several projects around Karanda are now in full swing, making this one of our busiest months yet. It has been exciting to see many of the projects we've been planning finally move from drawings and meetings into actual construction.

The new river settling tank continues to make great progress. As construction has picked up, Brianna has been spending a lot of time coordinating with the construction crew to make sure everything is being built according to plan. On an average day, she receives about three phone calls from the crew asking her to come to the site to answer questions, clarify details, or help solve unexpected challenges that arise. It has been rewarding to watch the project steadily take shape and know that it will improve the reliability of Karanda's water system.

Ben has been preparing to begin construction on a new tool organization system for the maintenance department. The goal is to make tools easier to find, organize, and track so the maintenance team can spend less time searching for equipment and more time getting work done.

For whatever reason, this month also became the "month of the pumps." It seemed like every day another pump somewhere around the hospital decided it had had enough. Thankfully, we have developed a pretty good repair routine. Brianna is usually the first to discover the problem, pull the pump out of whatever hole in the ground it happens to be living in, and deliver it to Ben. Ben then completely dismantles the pump, cleans it, repairs whatever has gone wrong, and carefully puts it back together. Once it is working again, Brianna heads back out to reinstall it. We have gone through this entire process at least four times this month, and at this point we're just waiting to see which pump decides to be next!

One thing we continue to appreciate is how well our jobs complement each other. It is fun to work together on projects like these, even if it sometimes means getting covered in mud while pulling pumps out of the ground. No two days at Karanda are ever the same, and while life here certainly keeps us on our toes, we are grateful for the opportunity to use our engineering backgrounds to serve the hospital and the surrounding community.





 

Time with Family:

  This month, Brianna's family came to visit us in Zimbabwe, and it was a nice time. They spent time getting to know our life here by visiting the hospital and even helping with a few small maintenance items. They also cooked dinner for us several times, which was especially appreciated because, after long days of work, we often do not have much energy left to cook.

We also had the opportunity to visit a nearby game park, where we met the owners, an incredible Christian family who have built a beautiful place here in Zimbabwe. While we were there, we found out that a baby tiger had just been born, the first tiger ever born in Zimbabwe! We were even able to play with it, which was an incredibly special and unexpected experience.

More than anything, we were grateful for the opportunity to share our life here with Brianna's family. Moving across the world and deciding to serve here long-term has understandably brought some fears and concerns for our families back home. We hope that having the opportunity to see where we live, meet the people we work with, visit the hospital, and experience a little bit of our daily life helped ease some of those fears. It meant so much to us to have them here, and we are incredibly grateful for the time we were able to spend together.
