BEN + BRI

#2 | JAN 2026

Monthly Newsletter

We are here!


We made it to Harare! It still feels surreal to say that - we are officially here and beginning this new chapter of ministry.

One of the biggest praises this month was our arrival process. At customs, it was as if God placed a shield around us. It seemed like everyone around us was being questioned, stopped, or had their luggage searched. We walked up to the counter, the officer stamped our passports, and waved us right through - no questions, no searches, nothing. It was such a clear reminder that God goes before us in ways we can’t always see.

Another huge blessing has been meeting a missionary who has walked this road before us. She came from the U.S. about 40 years ago and has been an incredible resource, helping us learn the culture, navigate daily life, and understand how ministry works here. We are so thankful God has placed someone like her in our lives.

And a fun praise: the grocery store we visited “just so happened” to add a gym section last month, so we were able to buy some dumbbells. <- (If you didn’t know the home gym is big deal for Brianna). It felt like such a small thing, but it was a big encouragement for us to maintain some normal rhythms in this new place.

Thank you Jesus for even the little things you care about.


 

What We Have Been Working On:

     We are both serving on the maintenance team, which means we support the hospital in many behind-the-scenes, but essential ways. Our work includes maintaining the water system, the wastewater system, and fixing things as they break… and when we say everything breaks, we truly mean everything.

Some days the power cuts out, which means learning how to work around interruptions and adjust plans on the fly. Other times, needed parts simply aren’t available in-country which requires patience, creativity, and a lot of problem-solving.

We are also slowly learning how things flow around here. The maintenance guys who work at the hospital have been kind and generous, taking time to show us what they work on day to day and how systems operate in this context. We are learning so much from their experience and practical knowledge.

At the same time, we have been able to bring in some of our own skills through our engineering and work experience. By working alongside the guys who know the hospital equipment, we can begin to help troubleshoot problems at a deeper level. It has been a really encouraging picture of teamwork, learning from one another and combining strengths to better serve the hospital.

     We are also beginning to teach some tech skills like Excel and computer use as the hospital aims to increase the accuracy and reliability of their inventory tracking and preventive maintenance.

While much of this work happens quietly, it plays a critical role in keeping the hospital running so that doctors, nurses, and staff can care for patients safely and effectively while showing them Christ’s love.


 

Spiritual Life:



We start everyday with devotions with the rest of the maintenance team which helps set our morning up right.

One unique rhythm here in Zimbabwe is daily “Tea Time” from 10:00a –10:30a. This has become a really special opportunity for us to slow down, sit with the maintenance guys, and simply talk. We’ve been able to hear about what’s going on in their lives, learn about their families, and build genuine relationships. Please continue praying for opportunities to share the gospel with these men as trust and deeper relationships continue to grow.

We’ve also been attending a Wednesday night Bible study, which has been incredibly filling and encouraging for us spiritually. On Sunday mornings, we help with kids at Awana (which is pure chaos 😅), and then we both attend the three-hour church service in Shona. Thankfully, we’re starting to pick up a few words here and there—but it’s definitely still a big challenge!


 

Challenges:



Adapting to the culture is a big hurdle starting out here.

Everything is different, the African culture is one thing, but even the things that seem western are based in British culture. Many things we expected, but it is always the little differences that get us the most.
We walk into grocery stores and don’t recognize a single brand (except Colgate toothpaste—praise the Lord for that).
We’ve had to completely rewrite our usual dinner recipes because many ingredients simply don’t exist here, and the ones we do find often taste different. For example, our “sausage and orzo” recipe has now become “chakalaka and couscous.”


On the technical side, one significant challenge we are currently facing involves the hospital’s wastewater system. At the moment, wastewater effluent is being released uphill from a borehole that once served as a reliable water source for the hospital. Over time, wastewater has seeped into the ground and contaminated that borehole, making it unusable. This is a complex issue with no quick fix, and it has highlighted just how critical proper infrastructure and long-term solutions are for the health and safety of the hospital.


 

Random Things We Have Learned:

  1. Just embrace the sweat

  2. Open doors and windows are non negotiable

  3. The best toilet paper comes from the vendors walking up and down the middle of the roads

  4. Greetings are extremely important, do not ask anyone anything work related unless you have first asked how they are doing


Thank you!


Thank you so much for wanting to be a part of of our lives. It is so humbling to know we have people who are genuinely interested in what God is doing in our lives.


We also want you to know that we genuinely welcome replies. Hearing from home will mean so much to us as we settle into a new country and culture. This move is a huge transition, and staying connected with our community back home is something we value deeply.


Please feel free to pass these updates along to anyone who might want to follow our journey. We will take all the prayer we can get.
