BEN + BRI

#3 | FEB 2026

Monthly Newsletter

Community Outreach


In addition to work within the hospital, we’ve been involved in broader community outreach efforts connected to Karanda Mission Hospital. Karanda serves 43 surrounding villages, representing roughly 4,000 households, many of which rely on limited or unsafe water sources.


We are currently in the process of planning and drilling new boreholes to improve access to clean water for these communities. While suitable technical locations have been identified, progress has at times been slowed by deeply held spiritual and cultural beliefs. In some villages, certain land is viewed as sacred, and permission to drill rests with village elders. In some cases, if a snake has been seen in an area, the land is immediately marked as superstitious and unsuitable for drilling, regardless of how ideal the location may be from a water-access standpoint.


This has placed us in a challenging position. In order to move forward, we are often faced with the responsibility of walking alongside village leaders and gently pointing them to the truth that God alone is sovereign, while also helping them understand the very real need for clean water for their community’s survival. These conversations require patience, humility, and great wisdom, as they touch both spiritual convictions and daily necessities.


We are learning that providing clean water is not just an engineering task, but an opportunity for Gospel-centered dialogue. Our prayer is that hearts would be softened, fear would give way to trust, and that these boreholes would become testimonies not only of physical provision, but of God’s faithfulness and care.



 

What We Have Been Working On:

   This month, much of our focus has been on strengthening the hospital’s water treatment process. We have been fine-tuning alum dosing, monitoring water quality, and investigating occasional issues like foam in the coagulation and sedimentation tanks, and the many river pump failures. Alongside this, we have continued documenting system procedures and exploring simple testing methods for bacteria, so the local lab team can perform them easily and consistently.


In addition, we have started several new projects aimed at improving both water quality and plant operations. We began a wastewater treatment project to better manage effluent and protect local waterways, and a settling tank project to improve sedimentation and reduce maintenance needs. To make daily operations easier, we are also adding simple control mechanisms so the water team doesn’t have to walk long distances to turn pumps on. These changes are helping the system run more efficiently and sustainably.



 

Spiritual Life:

One of the greatest privileges of serving here is seeing how physical care and eternal hope go hand in hand. Every single morning at 7:30am all around the hospital, doctors, nurses, maintenance guys, and patients hear the word of God from one of our chaplains. Doctors and nurses regularly pray with patients, and conversations about faith happen naturally in the midst of long days. We are continually reminded that while clean water is essential for physical healing, the deeper mission is sharing the hope of Christ.


This month, Brianna has started bringing her Bible during the morning water routine. There is about 20 minutes of downtime while waiting for the tanks to fill, and it is a perfect time for devotions. A little boy has begun lingering nearby and asking questions about this big book that she is reading. It has been a sweet reminder to just have our Bibles out more. Through some conversation, she has been able to explain that God is not just some big man who lives in the clouds, but God is here with us!


Another meaningful moment happened down by the river while running a percolation test. A man tending his garden came over and asked what she was doing. God helped guide the conversation toward faith. The man shared that he believed in the cross but was unsure how to get to heaven. Brianna was able to explain what it looks like to walk with God, and even with a slight language barrier, we pray that the message was clear.


Ben has also had opportunities to speak into deeper heart issues with some of the maintenance team. He has gently challenged cultural norms and attitudes toward women, helping them consider that just because it is their culture, does not mean it is how God desires us to act. These discussions are rooted in Scripture, respect, and humility, and we are encouraged by the openness and willingness to engage.



 

Challenges:




This month has highlighted how fragile water access can be. A major challenge has been the river pump, which provides most of the hospital’s water supply. Lately, it has been shutting off unpredictably—sometimes due to too little power, sometimes too much, sometimes no power at all, mechanical failure, and everything in between. Combined with occasional downtime of the solar pumps and contamination issues in some boreholes, there have been days when the hospital goes without water. These situations require quick problem-solving, careful planning, and resilience from the water team.
