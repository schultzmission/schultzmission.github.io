Monthly Newsletter

Community Outreach

     This past month was a busy and exciting one for community outreach. We were blessed once again by a family that has faithfully come to Karanda annually. Their commitment to this community is truly remarkable, and it has been interesting to see the relationships and traditions that have developed over nearly three decades of short term trips.

During their visit, we helped organize and host parties for orphans at five different schools throughout the district. Each event welcomed approximately 100 children who had been identified by their schools and invited to participate. In this context, "orphans" refers to children who have lost one or both of their parents. For many of these children, the day provided a rare opportunity to step away from the challenges of daily life and simply enjoy being kids.

The days were filled with activities, laughter, and plenty of energy. We played football (and were thoroughly reminded that the local kids are much better at it than we are), along with several other games and activities. We provided the food for each event, and village women prepared and cooked the meals that everyone enjoyed together. Alongside the fun, we also shared a Bible lesson and spent time discussing important health topics.

Before the children left, each one received a journal, fruit, and a large comforter blanket. We took special care to label each item with the child's name in the hope that it would help prevent the items from being lost or taken after they returned home. It was a simple step, but one that we hope will help ensure that the gifts remain a blessing to the children who received them.

As always, it was a joy to be part of these events and to witness the love and generosity shown to so many children throughout the district. We are grateful for this family that continues to return year after year, as well as for all those whose support makes opportunities like these possible. Through simple acts of fun, fellowship, practical gifts, and sharing God's love, we pray that these children were reminded that they are seen, valued, and deeply loved.


 

What We Have Been Working On:

    A big focus for us this month has been learning the language. While we have been studying for quite some time, we decided it was time to push ourselves a little harder. We’ve asked the maintenance team to speak Shona to us before they use English. This has left us confused plenty of times, but also opened the door for us to intentionally listen to more of the language and ask, “What does ___ mean?” plenty of times. It can be frustrating at times, but we know that immersion is one of the best teachers, and hopefully all of this effort will pay off in the long run.

The maintenance team seems to be enjoying the process almost as much as we are. They get a good laugh out of our pronunciation mistakes and creative attempts to communicate. Thankfully, they have been patient with us and have become some of our best language teachers. Every day brings a few new words, a few embarrassing mistakes, and hopefully a little bit more progress toward fluency.

On the work side of things, Ben has been splitting his time between longer project goals like the future power plan for Karanda and handling some of the shorter tasks that fall outside of the maintenance team’s usual work. One of the long-term goals is to help Karanda become more self-sufficient and less dependent on the national power grid that is regularly down. With the growth of the hospital, some of the power infrastructure is being pushed into failure so it is an important time to plan for improvements that will last through future development.

Brianna's workload has also continued to grow. She is currently overseeing the Water Department three days each week, which by itself is enough to keep her busy. On the remaining days, she has been coordinating with the construction crew for the new river settling tank project, working on designs for an expansion of the female ward, and evaluating options for adding an additional restroom to the ICU. Between managing water operations and juggling multiple construction and infrastructure projects, there is certainly no shortage of work to be done.




 

Spiritual Life:

  One thing that has been on both of our hearts recently has been the desire to build deeper relationships with people our own age. Living in Karanda is a tremendous blessing, but it can also be difficult to find peers, as many young adults are away at school or have moved to the cities in search of work.

We had been hoping to find a young adults group where we could build friendships and grow alongside others, but after realizing there wasn't one, we decided to start one ourselves. Together with another doctor our age, we have begun opening our home for any young adults who want to come and simply spend time together.

Our prayer is that our home would be a safe place where people can enjoy clean fun, build meaningful friendships, and know they have people they can turn to if they ever need encouragement or support. We share a short devotion each week, but our main focus right now is simply getting to know people and investing in relationships. It has already been such a sweet blessing for us, and we're excited to see how God continues to grow this new community. We imagine a Bible study may naturally develop in the future, but for now we're enjoying the opportunity to simply do life together.
